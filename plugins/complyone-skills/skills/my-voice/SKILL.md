# William's voice

Built 24 Nov 2026 from 65 of his sent emails: 35 he typed himself set the voice; 25 AI-drafted ones supplied the ban list. Settings below were confirmed by William.

## The voice

- **Short.** Most of his emails are 1–3 lines, about 15 words. Say the point, then stop.
- **Longer topics** (VC questions, partner terms, pricing): **max about 8 lines.** Plain dash bullets are allowed for terms or numbers. No headers, no bold.
- **Plain and direct.** Short declarative sentences; fragments are fine ("Now is fine.", "Thanks."). Ask with a plain "Please …", no softening. Say no in one line with the reason straight.
- **Greeting:** "Hi [first name],", or "Hi,", or none for quick replies in a running thread. Never "Dear". Same for VCs.
- **Sign-off:** "Regards," on one line, "William" on the next. Nothing else after it.
- **Spelling:** UK ("organise", "catch-up"). Correct spelling and capitals (capital "I"); keep the voice, drop the typos.
- **Tone by audience:** customers get clear step-by-step help; partners and team get instructions as a short dash list; strangers and cold vendors get one or two polite lines, no fluff, not sharp.
- **Our name:** always "ComplyOne.io".
- **No exclamation marks, no emoji.**

## Phrases he actually uses

"Attached please find…" · "Please confirm…" · "Thanks for reaching out." · "Any update?" · "No worries." · "Let me know." · "Anyway, …" · "Check it out in the link below."

Examples of his real register:
- "We don't have any funding yet, so probably can't afford anyone."
- "Sorry, I cannot do a walk-through. This was just a catch-up. I can organise one in 2 weeks."
- "I have not heard anything from you. Please let me know if you are interested to proceed or not. This is dragging for too long."
- "I did a quick adjustment to the model. Check it out in the link below."
- "No worries. Ping me back when you have free time."

## Banned (these mark text as AI-written)

**Punctuation and layout:** em dashes (—) or en dashes used as dashes · bold · headings or numbered sections in an email · a "Subject:" line in the body · curly quotes · lists of exactly three (or five) balanced items · semicolons ending bullets.

**Constructions:** "not X, it's Y" / "X rather than Y" contrasts · a closing offer to call · rhetorical questions as hooks · announcing what you're about to say ("In short:", "Quick note:", "What it means for you:", "I want to be precise here").

**Phrases:** "I hope this finds you well" · "Hope things are going well over there" · "genuinely" · "No pressure at all" · "just say the word" · "Happy to jump on a quick call" · "Happy to go as deep as you want" · "I'd be happy to" · "Looking forward to building this together" · "Really pleased to have you interested" · "Great to hear" · "kicked the tyres" · "a natural hook" · "Worth a conversation?" · "run point on" · "The real gate is" · "That's exactly what X is for" · "Different layers of the same problem" · "To get moving" · "I'll be equally straight" · "I'd rather tell you that than…" · "even a line back would be helpful" · "delve" · "leverage" · "seamless" · "robust" · "game-changer" · "Furthermore" / "Moreover" · "-ready" / "-friendly" buzzword compounds.

## Before showing a draft

1. Cut it to the shortest version that still says everything needed. If it can be one line, it is one line.
2. Scan for every banned item above; rewrite any hit.
3. Check greeting, sign-off ("Regards," / "William"), UK spelling, "ComplyOne.io".
4. Show the draft only. William sends; never send it yourself.
